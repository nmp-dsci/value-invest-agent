Extract the call — and the reasons that ladder up to it

Milestone 1 left 40 single-stock transcripts in transcript·lab and ten years of as-of prices and statements in DuckDB. Milestone 2 turns each transcript into a golden eval: BUY / HOLD / SELL, a target (intrinsic value), and the 3–5 reasons the author gives — each reason tagged with whether it can be rebuilt from the financial data available on the video date. The extraction runs as a versioned Agent SDK pipeline on the subscription, with a hand-labelled seed as its checkpoint, so it can be re-run and improved like the classifier was.

What the golden set is for

now after M1

  • vi.videos 40 in-sample, all exact dates, all US 10-K filers; transcripts in Chroma as raw_transcript:<id> + chunk:<id>:<i> with start_seconds
  • vi.prices from 2017, vi.statements EDGAR FY2010+ with real filed_at, yfinance fallback; vi.statements_as_of(tk, t0)

after M2 the deliverable

  • 120 GoldenEval rows (40 now, 80 after the window extension — D12): position BUY | HOLD | SELL (decided: fair value = HOLD; the binary "not a buy is a sell" view stays derivable), a 6-way stance_detail underneath it, expected return, intrinsic value with its scenario inputs
  • 3–5 ranked reasons, each a claim + verbatim quote + chunk id + timestamp + a data check: rebuilt from statements / prices / derived / external / judgement
  • A distilled method: valuation.py functions that reproduce his intrinsic values, and a summary of the parameters he actually uses
  • A checkpoint: seed labels, κ between two extractors, seed eval per extractor version, cache keyed on transcript hash
  • Golden Evals tab — validation insights on top (recommendation vs actual at T0 + 6 / 12 / 24 m, per year and split), every eval in a table below, review controls → the M2 review question: does the golden set make sense, and were the calls any good?
Why the reasons matter more than the label. With ~70 % of the 40 videos landing on SELL under the binary rule (§02) — 56 % if fair-holds are called HOLD (9 / 7 / 20) — a majority baseline already scores 56–70 % on position. The signal the analyst agent (M3) has to learn is how he gets there: which numbers he looks at, how he turns them into growth/multiple/probability inputs, and where he stops and says "too hard". That is what the reasons and the valuation block capture, and why every reason carries a data-check.

What the author actually does — read from the transcripts

Fourteen transcripts read end to end (AAPL ×2, VZ ×2, NVDA, TROW, UNH, TSLA, IBKR, CPB, BLK, CPRT, GOOGL, BRK ×2, AMZN, HSY, MU, MCD). The same procedure appears every time he values a stock, and it is fully reproducible: it is a ten-year two-stage earnings model with a terminal multiple, discounted at his required return, run as three scenarios with probabilities.

The reasons he gives, by kind — and how often they decide the call

valuation IV vs price · P/E vs growth

The decisive reason in almost every video: "intrinsic value 100, stock 228"; "P/E 26, if it reverts to 15 you're 50 % down" (MCD); "P/E 16 for a compounder that averaged 29" (CPRT). Rebuilt from prices + Diluted EPS.

growth trajectory and what's priced in

"Three years of practically no growth if it wouldn't be for buybacks" (AAPL); "revenue flat as a pancake" (VZ); "P/E went from 43 to 16 as growth went from double digit to zero" (CPRT). Rebuilt from revenue / EPS history; consensus growth is external.

capital allocation buybacks at what price, dividends, M&A

"Buyback yield 3.1 % at a 3.5 T market cap — stupid if done at a premium" (AAPL); "opportunistic buybacks when the stock is down — Buffett would applaud" (CPRT); "market cap lower than what they spent on acquisitions" (CPB). Rebuilt from Repurchase Of Capital Stock, Cash Dividends Paid, price × shares.

balance sheet · rates debt as the anchor

"Verizon is a pure interest-rate investment: 150 B of debt, refinancing 0.85 % paper at 5 %"; "the dividend yield is always ≈ 300 bp over the risk-free" (VZ). Debt and interest expense rebuilt; the risk-free rate is external (cheap to add).

"Semiconductors are clearly cyclical; when the cycle reverts it gets very ugly" (NVDA, MU); "index funds cost a tenth of their fees — the competitive advantage is gone" (TROW); "insurance goes to the too-hard pile" (UNH). Mostly judgement; partly visible in margin history.

02

His stance vocabulary, and the BUY / SELL rule

He almost never says "buy" or "sell" flat. He says where the stock sits on his quadrant and what he personally does. The headline label is BUY / HOLD / SELL (decided, D8): buys are BUY, fair-value holds are HOLD, avoid / too-hard / short are SELL. Your original binary — anything that is not a buy is a sell — is derived from it (HOLD → SELL) and reported beside it. Underneath, the extractor records the 6-way stance_detail and what he personally does, so any cut can be changed later without re-extracting.

The rule has an edge, and the edge is where the interesting videos are

A · strict binary derived view

BUY only when he says it is a buy (absolute or relative) or he is buying/accumulating it. Everything else — fair value, watchlist, avoid, too hard, short — is SELL.

On the 18 videos above: 6 BUY / 12 SELL. Across all 40 (provisional, §07): 9 BUY, 27 SELL, 4 pending.

Cost: VZ / CPRT / AMZN ("fairly valued for 10 %") become SELL although he'd hold them; BRK 2026 is BUY at a 6 % expected return.

B · hurdle

BUY when his own expected return at the current price ≥ 10 % (his discount rate), else SELL. Purely from the valuation block.

Cost: flips BRK 2026 to SELL and VZ/CPRT to borderline BUY; needs an IV in every video (≈ 3 of 40 have none, e.g. UNH).

C · 3-way headline: BUY / HOLD / SELL decided

BUY (absolute/relative buy) · HOLD (fair_hold) · SELL (avoid / too_hard / short). On the 36 labelled: 9 / 7 / 20 (+4 pending) — SELL is still the majority (56 %) but the set is far less lopsided than 9 / 27; per year 3/4/3 · 3/0/5 · 1/1/7 · 2/2/5. At 120 expect ≈ 27 / 21 / 67.

Preview verdicts at 12 m: BUY 3 ✓ / 2 ✗ (n 7) · HOLD 1 ✓ / 4 ✗ (n 5 — his "fairly valued" names moved > 10 pp either way: GOOG +33, MBUU −58) · SELL 8 ✓ / 6 ✗ (n 15). The HOLD band is the rule to think about: "did not lag by > 5 pp" may be the fairer test of a hold than "stayed within ±10 pp".

Decided (D8). Extract once, store stance_detail (6-way) + personal_action + expected_return_pct; derive position as BUY / HOLD / SELL (rule C) and report the binary A and the hurdle B as alternative cuts in the summary panel. Videos where the cuts disagree are flagged rule_sensitive=true so the review tab shows them first. The HOLD verdict band is D14.

One object per video. The call is what he concluded; the valuation is how he priced it; the reasons are why; the data checks say what an agent with only T0 data could have rebuilt. The spec's earlier fields (thesis[], risks[], verifiable_claims[]) collapse into reasons[] with a direction — a risk is a reason for SELL.

04

The pipeline: five stages, two of them model calls

Every model call goes through the existing llm.run_structured — a ClaudeSDKClient session with no tools, subscription_env(), one validated pydantic object back — exactly the shape the classifier used and tau2-loop's sdk_provider.run_query uses (including its wait-for-window-reset on a session-limit error, which I'll port). The extractor sees the transcript only. Market data enters in a deterministic Python stage, so a wrong number in the golden set can always be traced to either the author or the data, never to the model reading both.

06

Distillation: from 40 calls to functions and parameters

Because the valuation is one model with a handful of judgement inputs, "learning his approach" decomposes into (a) code that is right by construction and (b) a small table of the judgements he tends to make. Both are M2 deliverables; M3's agents/v0/helper.py starts as (a) plus loaders, and M3's system.md starts as (b) in prose.

src/value_invest/valuation.pythe template, as code · tested against §01
def two_stage_iv(base, g1, g2, terminal_multiple, r=0.10, payout=0.0, years=10):
    """His intrinsic-value template: base grows g1 for 5 years then g2 for 5,
    terminal value = E10 × multiple, dividends at payout ratio, all discounted at r.
    Exact cell layout pinned in S4.0 from the downloadable template."""
    e, pv_div = base, 0.0
    for t in range(1, years + 1):
        e *= (1 + g1) if t <= 5 else (1 + g2)
        pv_div += e * payout / (1 + r) ** t
    return e * terminal_multiple / (1 + r) ** years + pv_div

What the summary will show (numbers filled by vi golden summary)

  • Discount rate — 10 % in all 8 template runs among the 14 read; he moves it only to express a target ("18 % if the bull case")
  • Terminal multiples — slow growers 15–20 normal / 10–12 worst; growth names 20–25 normal / 25–40 best; BRK 15–25
  • Probabilities — 70/15/15 or 50/25/25; he raises the worst-case weight when uncertainty rises (GOOGL: "increasing the worst case given the high investment")
  • Hurdle — BUY language appears when expected return ≥ ~10 % and he can name a margin of safety; fair value at 10 % is a hold
  • Reason → input — growth reasons feed g₁; cyclicality/competition feed the worst-case multiple and probability; capital allocation adjusts the base (buyback-adjusted EPS growth); rates feed the dividend model's terminal yield

Risks, and the decisions — all taken

Decisions D8–D14 are recorded in ai_specs/s02_m2_golden_extraction.md and in the project plan (rev 4). Build starts with S4.0 on branch m2-golden.

high Title ≠ transcript — the transcript is the source of truth

BLK ("Buy… for a quick 2x!") is ironic; AMZN/CPRT titles say buy, he holds. med Class imbalance

med Hedged stance → low κ

med Template ambiguity

±10 % rows in §01. Control: S4.0 pins the spreadsheet before any extraction; IV fidelity is a gate metric.

low Transcript noise
